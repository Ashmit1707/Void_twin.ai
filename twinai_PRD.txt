DigitalTwin.ai — Product Requirements Document (PRD)
Hackathon Round 2 — Predictive Digital Twin for Vehicle Assembly Lines

============================================================
1. PRODUCT OVERVIEW
============================================================

DigitalTwin.ai is a web-based predictive digital twin for mixed-model vehicle assembly lines.

The system accepts historical, simulated, or illustrative production data in CSV format, processes the data through a backend ML pipeline, and generates a station-level view of the assembly line showing where bottlenecks are forming and how bottleneck risk changes over time.

The prototype focuses on one clear demonstration:

CSV Upload
    ↓
Data Validation & Preprocessing
    ↓
Production Data Simulation / Augmentation
    ↓
ML Prediction Engine
    ↓
Bottleneck Risk Calculation
    ↓
Interactive Digital Twin Visualization
    ↓
Station-level Analysis

The goal is NOT to build a production-ready factory management system. The goal is to build a convincing, functional proof-of-concept that demonstrates the core predictive mechanism and clearly addresses the Round 2 problem statement.


============================================================
2. PROBLEM STATEMENT
============================================================

Modern vehicle assembly plants contain a mixture of legacy and modern equipment.

Some stations may provide detailed telemetry such as:
- Cycle time
- Torque
- Temperature
- Vibration
- Machine state
- Throughput

Other stations may depend on:
- Manual checklists
- Operator observations
- Periodic inspections
- Limited production records

This makes traditional predictive systems difficult to deploy across an entire assembly line.

Bottlenecks are also intermittent and multi-causal. A station may appear healthy most of the time but begin accumulating queues because of increasing cycle time, reduced efficiency, upstream variation, equipment degradation, or environmental conditions.

A defect or production issue introduced early in the line may only become visible much later.

DigitalTwin.ai aims to provide a single predictive view of the production line that identifies stations at increasing risk of becoming bottlenecks before the bottleneck becomes severe.

The prototype should explicitly demonstrate that useful predictions can still be made when sensor coverage is incomplete.


============================================================
3. PRIMARY PRODUCT GOAL
============================================================

The central question the prototype should answer is:

"Can we use imperfect production data to identify which assembly stations are likely to become bottlenecks before the bottleneck becomes severe?"

A successful demo should allow a judge to go from raw CSV data to an understandable production-line visualization within a few minutes.

Success criteria:

1. User can upload a CSV.
2. System validates and processes the data.
3. Backend generates or predicts bottleneck risk.
4. Risk varies across stations and over time.
5. User can visually identify high-risk stations.
6. User can inspect the underlying production metrics.
7. User can sort stations by different metrics.
8. System can explain why a station has elevated risk.
9. Missing sensor data does not cause the entire pipeline to fail.
10. The demo clearly communicates predictive rather than merely descriptive analytics.

============================================================
9. ML / PREDICTION MECHANISM
============================================================

The ML component must produce a meaningful Bottleneck Risk Score for every station and timestamp.

Risk scale:

0   = Very Low Risk
100 = Very High Risk

Example:

Station       Risk
Body-04        18
Body-07        34
Paint-12       61
Final-03       87

The frontend converts this score into the green-to-red visualization.

The model should aim to detect increasing risk rather than only identify already-existing bottlenecks.

Example:

Current Risk:   62
Predicted Risk: 84
Trend:          ↑ +22


============================================================
10. FEATURE ENGINEERING
============================================================

Potential production features:

- Cycle time
- Takt time
- Queue time
- Throughput
- Utilisation

Potential equipment features:

- Temperature
- Vibration
- Torque
- Machine state

Potential temporal features:

- Rolling average cycle time
- Cycle-time deviation
- Queue growth
- Utilisation trend
- Previous station performance
- Historical station baseline

Derived features:

Takt deviation:
cycle_time / takt_time

Queue growth:
current_queue - previous_queue

Cycle-time instability:
rolling standard deviation of cycle_time

Other derived features may be added if they improve prediction quality and remain explainable.


============================================================
11. HANDLING MISSING SENSORS
============================================================

The system must NOT assume every station has every sensor.

Possible strategies:

1. Statistical Imputation
Use historical station data where appropriate.

2. Derived Signals
Estimate station health from available production variables such as:
- Queue time
- Cycle time
- Utilisation
- Throughput

3. Feature Availability Awareness
The model should know which signals are actually available.

4. Prediction Confidence
Every prediction can optionally include a confidence score.

Example:

Bottleneck Risk: 82
Prediction Confidence: 74%

Suggested product message:

"The digital twin does not require perfect instrumentation. It adapts its prediction confidence based on the quality and availability of station data."

Queue Time ↑
Utilisation ↑
Risk ↑

18.3 Sudden Equipment Issue

Temperature ↑
Vibration ↑

Queue ↑
Risk ↑

18.4 Upstream Issue

Upstream station risk ↑
        ↓
Downstream queue ↑
        ↓
Downstream bottleneck risk ↑

This demonstrates the multi-station and multi-causal nature of production problems.

============================================================
24. ML IMPLEMENTATION DIRECTION
============================================================

Do not overcomplicate the model.

A practical MVP can combine:

- Isolation Forest for anomaly detection
- Statistical Process Control for deviations from normal behaviour
- Rolling-window trend analysis
- Interpretable risk scoring

Example conceptual risk:

Risk =
    0.30 × cycle-time deviation
  + 0.25 × queue growth
  + 0.20 × utilisation
  + 0.15 × process anomaly
  + 0.10 × upstream risk

IMPORTANT:
These weights are illustrative starting points, not scientifically validated constants.

The implementation team should calibrate or learn the weights using the available simulated/illustrative data.

The goal is not to claim industrial-grade predictive accuracy.

The goal is to demonstrate a coherent predictive mechanism that can later be validated against real production outcomes.


============================================================
25. PREDICTIVE VS DESCRIPTIVE ANALYTICS
============================================================

The project should emphasize prediction.

Weak:
"Station 12 is currently a bottleneck."

Stronger:
"Station 12 shows a rapidly increasing probability of becoming a bottleneck in the next prediction window."

Dashboard example:

Current Risk       62
Predicted Risk     84
Trend              ↑ +22

The exact prediction horizon should be defined by the implementation based on the generated dataset.

============================================================
28. LEGACY SYSTEM / SAFETY APPROACH
============================================================

The prototype should explicitly avoid modifying live production control logic.

Recommended deployment concept:

Existing PLC / SCADA / MES / sensors
              ↓
        Read-only data
              ↓
       DigitalTwin.ai
              ↓
       Prediction / Alerts

The system should be presented as a monitoring and decision-support layer rather than a system that directly controls the assembly line.

This reduces operational risk and addresses the challenge's requirement to work around infrequent maintenance windows.

32. CORE PRODUCT PRINCIPLE
============================================================

The prototype should follow one central principle:

MAKE THE PREDICTION UNDERSTANDABLE.

A judge should be able to answer three questions immediately:

1. WHERE is the problem?
   → Station-level risk visualization.

2. WHEN is it becoming a problem?
   → Risk changing over time.

3. WHY does the model think it is a problem?
   → Queue, takt, utilisation, anomalies, upstream effects, and confidence.

- Professional
- Technical
- Clean
- Data-dense but understandable
- Dark or dark-neutral industrial aesthetic is acceptable

============================================================
36. FINAL HACKATHON POSITIONING
============================================================

DigitalTwin.ai is a predictive decision-support layer for vehicle assembly lines that works even when production data is incomplete.

Instead of waiting for a bottleneck to become visible, DigitalTwin.ai continuously evaluates station behaviour and identifies where bottleneck risk is increasing.

The core differentiator is:

"Predict the bottleneck before the line feels it."

The prototype demonstrates this using realistic/simulated production data while explicitly addressing uneven sensor coverage, temporal behaviour, explainability, and safe read-only integration with existing industrial systems.
